## 1. Product
Synthetic acceptance adaptation: demo.py is a documented example, not a production app or customer event.
FocusFence is a Slack and Google Calendar app that auto-declines meeting requests dropped into a person's focus blocks, and replies with the requester's own stated priority list so the requester sees why they got declined. Corrected by the builder: the auto-decline only fires after a 15 minute grace window, not instantly.

## 2. Audience and venues
For engineering managers and senior ICs at 50 to 500 person companies who already block "focus time" on their calendar but get it double-booked anyway. They gather on r/ExperiencedDevs (https://example.com/r/experienceddevs) and in the #no-meetings channel of the Rands Leadership Slack (https://example.com/rands-slack). Verbatim complaint, r/ExperiencedDevs: "I block Tuesday mornings every week and someone always finds a way to book over it, and then I'm the jerk for declining." (https://example.com/complaint-1)

## 3. Category default
`unverified`, competitor research was not run for this fixture.
Reclaim.ai, Clockwise, and Calendly all pitch "smart scheduling" and "protect your time" in nearly the same sentence, all show a calm pastel calendar screenshot as the hero image, and all avoid ever naming who is doing the over-booking.

## 4. Taste
Love, the r/ExperiencedDevs complaint thread: "because it's the first time I've seen someone admit the guilt is the actual product of a bad calendar, not the double-booking itself."
Hate, Reclaim.ai's launch video: "because it never once says the word 'no,' the whole video is about the calendar being nice about it."
Bar description, verbatim: "it's the app that tells your coworker no so you don't have to."
Origin story, verbatim: "I built this after declining my own skip-level's meeting by accident and getting a Slack message about it within four minutes."
What annoys the builder about the category, verbatim: "every one of these tools talks about time like it's a resource you optimize, not a boundary you enforce."

## 5. Direction
Name: The Bouncer. Enemy: the calendar tools that make protecting your time feel like a scheduling feature instead of a boundary. Kind of unsafe: FocusFence's pieces say "no" in the product's own voice, in public, instead of hiding the decline behind polite copy. Recurring move: every piece ends with the exact output of the local demo export, explicitly labelled Demo reply. Three things it can say that competitors cannot: it names the specific meeting that got declined, it admits the builder's own skip-level got declined once, and it treats "no" as a feature to advertise rather than friction to smooth over. What it is willing to lose: readers who manage other people's calendars for a living, and any comment calling the tool "rude." Provocation and finding: built from the r/ExperiencedDevs complaint that the guilt of declining is the real product gap (section 2).

## 6. Rules for pieces
One strong opinion per piece. The recurring move, the labelled local demo export, appears in every piece. The spine is always one of the three things, usually the specific declined meeting.
