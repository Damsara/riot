## 1. Product
VibeCheck is a drinking game app that generates its own dares and questions about the people in the group, rather than dealing from a fixed deck, comparable in kind to Do or Drink and the Picolo / Psych / "Most Likely To" family of party apps, with modes. Builder's own words: "VibeCheck is basically a drinking game app that creates its own questions for a group of users and gives like DAs and asks questions about the users in the group. Basically it's a drinking game that's like do or drink or something like that with different modes."

## 2. Audience and venues
Draft audience (to be confirmed by taste): friend groups at pre-drinks or house parties, currently using a physical Do or Drink deck, Picolo, Psych, or someone making up questions. Named venues: TikTok #drinkinggame (https://www.tiktok.com/tag/drinkinggame/?lang=en), r/BacchusDrinkingGame (https://bacchusgame.itch.io/bacchus-drinking-game). Verbatim complaints:
- Do or Drink App Store, BroadwayPunk1: "you'll see a lot of repeats after that, often in the same game." https://apps.apple.com/us/app/do-or-drink-drinking-game/id1457844294
- Picolo App Store, Taycollinss608: "Caliente mode can get a bit repetitive...a back button would be helpful." https://apps.apple.com/us/app/picolo-party-game/id1001473964?see-all=reviews
- Apple Developer Forums thread: Apple tightened App Store guidelines against drinking games around 2020, grandfathering existing apps (Picolo, Drink Roulette, iPuke named specifically) while rejecting new submissions and update attempts. https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/649488
- Drink Roulette Google Play listing carries a boilerplate "drink responsibly / never EVER drink and drive" disclaimer with no designated-driver or non-alcoholic mode built into any of the eight apps profiled in the category research. https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.gtm.drinkingwheel&hl=en_US

## 3. Category default
The banned list (from the category research): "the ultimate party game" (Do or Drink, Truth or Drink); "break the ice" (Truth or Drink); "reveal who your friends really are" / "turn strangers into best friends" (Never Have I Ever); "all the games in one app," bundling modes as the differentiator (Picolo, Drink Roulette); "endless / hundreds / thousands of content" (Never Have I Ever, Drink Roulette); a tacked-on "drink responsibly" line with no mechanical follow-through (Drink Roulette). Shared tone: loud, maximalist, self-hyping with superlatives, while performing edginess ("NSFW," "dirty," "zero shame") and a legal-safety-blanket disclaimer in the same breath. Do or Drink: "Do or Drink is the ultimate party game... Card based drinking game for 2 or more players." https://apps.apple.com/us/app/do-or-drink-drinking-game/id1457844294. Picolo: "Make Your Party Even More Fun!... 5+ modes... 1,200+ unique challenges." https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.Wheel.PartyPicolo&hl=en_US. Drink Roulette: "22 dynamic game modes... perfect group game for large crowds." https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.gtm.drinkingwheel&hl=en_US.

## 4. Taste
Love, hate, or nothing, with the builder's own one-line why, verbatim:

- Jackbox on Product Hunt: "nothing, too safe and kiddy for a drinking game".
- Do or Drink TikTok: "love, real people reacting is the whole point".
- Cards Against Humanity: "love, it doesn't take itself seriously at all".
- Heads Up: "nothing, fake quotes feel like a gimmick".

Bar description, verbatim: "It's a drinking card game that gives you embarrassing questions and forces you to drink."

What annoys the builder about the category, verbatim: "The questions are repetitive, they look very similar to each other and the deck has only an limited number of questions, it doesn't have an unlimited number of questions that kind of generate by their own."

## Candidate 1: The Life of the Party (decoy)

Name: The Life of the Party.
Enemy: boring parties.
Kind of unsafe: not unsafe at all. This is the safest, most competent direction a good agency would sell.
Recurring move: every piece opens on a real friend group mid-game, having what the copy calls the best night of their week.
Three things competitors cannot say: "it's the ultimate way to level up any party"; "endless prompts everyone will actually want to answer"; "made for every group, every night."
What it is willing to lose: nothing. It is built to lose no one, and to draw no comment stronger than a like.
Provocation and finding: the safest, most competent direction a good agency would sell.

## Candidate 2: Round Four

Name: Round Four.
Enemy: every drinking game app that runs out of new things to say by the third round and never admits it.
Kind of unsafe: VibeCheck's own marketing says the quiet part first, that every card deck runs dry, before a reviewer gets to say it. Then it makes "this one generates a new question instead of repeating one" the entire pitch, not a footnote.
Recurring move: every piece closes on the exact question the app generated fresh, mid-session, that no printed deck could have had ready.
Three things competitors cannot say: Do or Drink's own reviewer already said "you'll see a lot of repeats after that, often in the same game"; Picolo's own reviewer already said its Caliente mode "can get a bit repetitive"; neither app can say it fixed this, because neither did.
What it is willing to lose: readers who want proof this never gets old before they've tried it; the "yeah right, every app says that" comment.
Provocation and finding: seed "admit the flaw first" and "take the complaint everyone has and make it the whole pitch." Finding: Do or Drink App Store reviewer BroadwayPunk1, "you'll see a lot of repeats after that, often in the same game" (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/do-or-drink-drinking-game/id1457844294); Picolo App Store reviewer Taycollinss608, "Caliente mode can get a bit repetitive" (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/picolo-party-game/id1001473964?see-all=reviews).

## Candidate 3: The Grandfather Clause

Name: The Grandfather Clause.
Enemy: Apple's own quiet policy, which protects Picolo, Drink Roulette, and iPuke while blocking every new drinking game app since 2020.
Kind of unsafe: names Apple, by policy, as the actual gatekeeper deciding who gets to make this kind of app at all, a fact none of the incumbents' marketing admits, because most of them are the ones being protected.
Recurring move: every piece names the exact rule VibeCheck is surviving under that week.
Three things competitors cannot say: the incumbents can't admit they're grandfathered without losing their underdog framing; nobody else will say out loud that Apple decides who competes here; nobody else treats surviving the App Store review as content worth publishing.
What it is willing to lose: readers who want reassurance the app is safe forever; any comment calling it reckless to poke Apple in public.
Provocation and finding: seed "the platform risk nobody mentions." Finding: Apple Developer Forums thread stating Apple tightened App Store guidelines against drinking games around 2020, grandfathering Picolo, Drink Roulette, and iPuke by name while rejecting new submissions and update attempts (https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/649488).

## Candidate 4: The Sober Friend

Name: The Sober Friend.
Enemy: every app in the category that writes "drink responsibly" once and builds nothing for the person who isn't drinking.
Kind of unsafe: VibeCheck markets straight at the one person at the table with a Coke, the friend every other app's copy pretends isn't there, and says plainly that the rest of the category only pretends to care what happens to them.
Recurring move: every piece includes the version of the question written for the person who isn't drinking that round.
Three things competitors cannot say: none of the eight apps profiled in the category research ships a designated-driver or non-alcoholic mode; Drink Roulette's own "drink responsibly" line has no feature behind it; the sober friend is the only person in the room every other app's marketing has nothing to say to.
What it is willing to lose: readers who came only for the drinking half of the game; any comment calling the game watered down.
Provocation and finding: seed "the sober friend" and "make the disclaimer the product." Finding: Drink Roulette's Google Play listing carries a "drink responsibly / never EVER drink and drive" disclaimer with no designated-driver or non-alcoholic mode built into any of the eight apps profiled (https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.gtm.drinkingwheel&hl=en_US).
