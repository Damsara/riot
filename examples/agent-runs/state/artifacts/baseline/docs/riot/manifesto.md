## 1. Product
VibeCheck is a drinking game app that generates its own dares and questions about the people in the group, rather than dealing from a fixed deck, comparable in kind to Do or Drink and the Picolo / Psych / "Most Likely To" family of party apps, with modes. Builder's own words: "VibeCheck is basically a drinking game app that creates its own questions for a group of users and gives like DAs and asks questions about the users in the group. Basically it's a drinking game that's like do or drink or something like that with different modes."

## 2. Audience and venues
Draft audience (to be confirmed by taste): friend groups at pre-drinks or house parties, currently using a physical Do or Drink deck, Picolo, Psych, or someone making up questions. Named venues: TikTok #drinkinggame (https://www.tiktok.com/tag/drinkinggame/?lang=en), r/BacchusDrinkingGame (https://bacchusgame.itch.io/bacchus-drinking-game). Verbatim complaints:

# VibeCheck: nine objections

Written by hand for the cop-brief dry run (RIOT-16), as stance rung 5 round one would produce it: each unsafe candidate's author writes one paragraph per rival, attack only, the single strongest reason the audience would ignore it, no defence. The decoy has no author writing attacks; three unsafe authors times three rivals gives nine.

## Round Four's author, on The Life of the Party

It says "endless prompts everyone will actually want to answer" in the same breath every other app in this category already uses, and the audience has seen "endless" and "hundreds" on every listing they've already ignored. Nobody scrolling past Do or Drink for the fifth time stops for a fresher coat of the same word.

## Round Four's author, on The Grandfather Clause

Naming the App Store's grandfather clause is a builder-forum story, not a party story; the person about to play tonight does not care which app survived a 2020 policy change, they care whether the deck run out by round three. This candidate answers a question nobody at the table is asking.

## Round Four's author, on The Sober Friend

Leading with the one person not drinking undersells the product to the group actually opening the app, who came to drink; a pitch that spends its first line reassuring the sober friend reads as an apology for the game before anyone has played a round.

## The Grandfather Clause's author, on The Life of the Party

"It's the ultimate way to level up any party" is the exact sentence Do or Drink and Truth or Drink already use; a scroller who has seen "ultimate" twice this week has no reason to read a third one, however well the agency wrote it.

## The Grandfather Clause's author, on Round Four

Admitting the deck repeats and then promising it's fixed is the same move every subscription-app apology post makes right before a price hike; the audience has learned to read "we fixed the repetition" as the sentence right before a paywall, not as a reason to trust the fix.

## The Grandfather Clause's author, on The Sober Friend

A designated-driver mode is a feature announcement, not a reason to open the app tonight; it reads like a wellness pivot, and a scroller looking for a drinking game moves past anything that sounds like it's about to lecture them.

## The Sober Friend's author, on The Life of the Party

"Made for every group, every night" is the line every competitor's landing page already promises and none of them deliver on, per the same repetition complaints this research turned up; restating the promise without fixing what breaks it just puts this candidate in line behind the apps the audience already resents.

## The Sober Friend's author, on Round Four

Turning "the deck repeats" into the whole pitch hands the audience the exact worry they had about every other app in the category and asks them to trust that this one is different with no proof beyond the claim; a scroller who has been burned by "we added more cards" updates before does not stop for another one.

## The Sober Friend's author, on The Grandfather Clause

Telling a first-time player that the app is one Apple policy change from disappearing is a reason to screenshot the deck and never open the app again, not a reason to start a round; platform risk is a founder's worry, and putting it in the marketing hands the audience a worry they didn't have before they clicked.
