## What gets you punished

- A weak hook, the first tweet on X or the first slide on LinkedIn, kills the whole piece: the rest sits behind "Show this thread" or an unswiped carousel, and a hook that doesn't earn the tap or the swipe loses the reach before content is seen.
- A link in the hook tweet triggers a reported 30 to 50% reach cut on X.
- Padding a thread past about 10 tweets, or a carousel past its natural idea length, trades reach for length as completion rates fall on every extra unit.
- Mixed page sizes in a carousel's source file block the LinkedIn upload outright until corrected.
- Engagement bait ("reply and I'll follow everyone back," "agree?" polls, comment-to-unlock asks) is a named, separately enforced policy on both X and LinkedIn, and repeated solicitation pulls an X account from the creator revenue-share program.
- Coordinated or bought engagement, follower farms, reply rings, sock puppets, engagement pods, is banned by name on both platforms.

## Draft rules

1. The hook is the first line and stands alone; it must work with nothing before or after it.
2. One beat per line: each tweet or slide carries exactly one idea, never a fragment that only makes sense with the next one.
3. The last line restates the opinion, not a call to action, unless the occasion is a launch.
4. Every claim carries a source URL or is marked `unverified`.
